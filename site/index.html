STOCK

Ending pending

Opening pending + instituted − disposed

Monthly end stock is a point-in-time measure. It is not summed across months.

RATIO

Pendency

Ending pending ÷ opening pending

Describes the stock relative to opening pending; this is distinct from clearance.

RATIO

Clearance

Disposed ÷ instituted

Can exceed 100% when disposals outnumber institutions in the selected period.

RATIO

Adjournment

Adjourned hearings ÷ listed hearings

Hearing-level ratio. It does not identify the cause of an adjournment.

AGE PROFILE

Long-pending share

Cases over 3 or 5 years ÷ ending pending

Uses the latest available month-end age snapshot; age bands must be nested.

ROLLING WINDOW

Calendar 3-month metrics

Aggregate flows ÷ aggregate flows

Stocks use first-month opening and final-month ending. Age uses final-month snapshot. Missing calendar months or changing grain coverage make the window unavailable.

QUALITY CHECKS

Invalid records stop the build.

Inputs require all schema columns and values, non-negative integer counts, valid calendar months, consistent age bands, no impossible disposal or hearing counts, and unique state × district × court type × category × month keys. CSVs containing personal-data column names are refused before rows are parsed. Rejection logs include safe aggregate values and reasons.

WHY IT EXISTS

Make the method visible.

The project shows how an aggregate court-data dashboard can document sources, validate input, explain formulas, and avoid treating stocks and flows as interchangeable.

OFFICIAL CONTEXT

Start with primary data.

For real research, consult current coverage and definitions directly from the National Judicial Data Grid (NJDG) and source documentation. This demo does not download NJDG data.

KNOWN LIMITS

Metrics are descriptive.

Aggregate ratios cannot explain why cases take time, what caused a hearing to adjourn, or whether a court is performing well. Coverage and reporting practices can vary across jurisdictions.

DATA BOUNDARY

No personal or crime data.

The pipeline rejects personal-data column names and does not include a crime series. IPC/BNS comparisons require an explicit crosswalk and careful treatment of the July 2024 transition.
